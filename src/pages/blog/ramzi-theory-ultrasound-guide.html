Ramzi Theory: How to Read Your Ultrasound for Gender Prediction

Published August 7, 2026 · Gender Predictor Team

What Is the Ramzi Theory?

The Placenta Position Method

The Ramzi theory (also called the Ramzi method) is a gender prediction approach that claims to determine a baby's sex as early as 6 weeks of pregnancy by looking at the location of the placenta on an early ultrasound. Dr. Saad Ramzi published a study in 2011 suggesting a link between placenta placement and fetal gender, and the theory has since become one of the most popular gender prediction methods online.

Key claim: If the placenta is on the right side of the uterus, it's a boy. If it's on the left side, it's a girl. Simple, but is it true?

How the Ramzi Theory Works

Reading Your 6-Week Ultrasound

During a transvaginal ultrasound at 6 to 8 weeks, the sonographer can identify the chorionic villi (the future placenta). According to Ramzi:

  • Left-side placenta = Girl
  • Right-side placenta = Girl

Wait -- that doesn't seem right. Actually, the theory says:

  • Placenta on the right = Boy
  • Placenta on the left = Girl

The key is that you need to know which side the placenta is on relative to your body, not relative to the ultrasound image (which may be mirrored depending on the probe orientation). This is the most common mistake people make when trying to apply the Ramzi method at home.

How to Read Your Ultrasound for Ramzi

If you're looking at your own ultrasound image, here's a step-by-step guide:

  1. Identify the gestational sac -- this is the dark, round area containing the embryo.
  2. Find the bright area on one side of the sac -- this is the chorionic villi (future placenta).
  3. Determine which side it's on relative to your body. If the ultrasound is transvaginal, left on screen typically equals right side of your body (mirrored). If abdominal, it may not be mirrored.
  4. Apply the rule: placenta on right = boy, placenta on left = girl.

If you're unsure about the orientation, ask your sonographer or doctor. They can tell you which side the placenta is forming on.

Is the Ramzi Theory Scientifically Proven?

No, the Ramzi theory is not scientifically proven. Here are the key facts:

  • Dr. Ramzi's 2011 study has never been published in a peer-reviewed medical journal.
  • The study claimed 97% accuracy but provided no methodology that can be independently verified.
  • No replication studies have confirmed the findings.
  • Most medical professionals and organizations (ACOG, NHS, etc.) do not recognize the Ramzi method as a valid predictor.
  • A 2014 study published in the Journal of Obstetrics and Gynaecology Canada found no correlation between placental location and fetal sex.

Why Do People Believe It?

The Ramzi theory is popular because it promises an answer weeks before NIPT blood tests or anatomy scans can confirm gender. For expectant parents counting down the days, any early sign feels valuable. Combined with confirmation bias (remembering the times it was right, forgetting when it was wrong), it's easy to see why the theory persists.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Ramzi theory peer-reviewed?

No. The placenta-side claim was never validated by independent peer-reviewed studies and is not accepted by mainstream obstetrics.

How early does Ramzi claim to work?

Six to eight weeks, from the side of the uterus the placenta attaches to — but this is about chance, and scan images are often mirrored.

What is the earliest reliable test instead?

A cell-free DNA NIPT blood test from around 10 weeks reports sex with better than 99 percent accuracy where disclosure is legal.
