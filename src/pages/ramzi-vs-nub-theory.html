Ramzi Theory vs Nub Theory: Which Ultrasound Gender Prediction Is More Reliable?

Both Ramzi Theory and Nub Theory use early pregnancy ultrasound images to guess baby gender. But they work at different stages of pregnancy (6-8 weeks vs 11-14 weeks), look at different anatomical features, and have dramatically different accuracy rates. Here's the definitive comparison.

Why Nub Theory Is More Reliable

Unlike Ramzi theory, nub theory is based on real anatomy. The genital tubercle is the precursor to external genitalia, and it begins angling differently for males vs females between 11-14 weeks. Multiple independent studies have confirmed 70-91% accuracy — making nub theory the most accurate non-medical method available. At 13-14 weeks with a clear image, accuracy can exceed 90%.

Ramzi Theory: Earlier, But Unproven

Ramzi theory's main advantage is timing — at 6-8 weeks, it's the earliest possible gender guess. But the science simply isn't there. Dr. Ramzi's 2011 study was never peer-reviewed or replicated. Most OB/GYNs don't even know what Ramzi theory is, and those who do consider it entertainment, not medicine.
