Baking Soda Gender Test: Complete Guide

A spoon of baking soda, a cup of morning urine and a lot of hope. The baking soda gender test is one of the most viral at-home predictions: if it fizzes, you're having a boy; if it sits flat, a girl.

Quick Answer

Mix first-morning urine with baking soda — fizzing is said to mean boy, no reaction a girl. The fizz is just a base meeting the mild acid in urine and releasing CO₂. Urine acidity swings with your hydration, diet and timing, not your baby’s sex, so the test is safe but accurate only about 50% of the time.

How to Do the Baking Soda Test

  1. Collect a small cup of first-morning urine (most concentrated).
  2. Add about a teaspoon of plain baking soda.
  3. Do not stir immediately — watch for a minute.
  4. Bubbling and fizzing is read as a boy; staying still as a girl.

The Chemistry Behind the Reaction

Baking soda is sodium bicarbonate, a base. When a base meets an acid it releases carbon dioxide gas — the bubbles you see. Normal urine is mildly acidic, typically around pH 5 to 7, and that acidity changes a lot through the day and across pregnancies based on how much water you drank, what you ate, and how long since you last urinated. There is no biological reason a boy or a girl would change your urine pH, so the fizz is telling you about your hydration, not your baby.

Accuracy: About 50%

Because the reaction is driven by factors unrelated to sex, the test cannot beat a coin flip. That is the same 50% ceiling shared by the Chinese chart, the ring test and the symptoms quiz. The only reliable answers are NIPT or the anatomy scan.

Is It Safe?

Completely. It uses only urine and baking soda in a cup — nothing is ingested or applied to your body, so there is no risk to you or the baby. Wash your hands afterwards and go about your day.

Why Urine pH Varies (and Why That Is All the Test Reads)

  • Hydration: more water dilutes urine and reduces fizz.
  • Diet: citrus, protein and coffee all shift acidity.
  • Timing: first-morning urine is the most concentrated.
  • The amount of baking soda you add changes how dramatic the reaction looks.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you do the baking soda gender test?

Add a spoon of baking soda to first-morning urine: fizzing is said to mean boy, no reaction a girl.

Does the baking soda gender test work?

No. Fizzing is baking soda reacting with urine acid, which reflects diet and hydration — not sex — so it's accurate about half the time.

Is it safe?

Yes, nothing touches your body; just wash your hands after.

Why does my urine make it fizz?

Urine is mildly acidic and baking soda is a base, so they release CO₂ bubbles — more so the more acidic your urine is that day.
