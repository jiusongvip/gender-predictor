Deep Dive: Medical Methods

NIPT (Non-Invasive Prenatal Testing)

NIPT is a blood test that analyzes cell-free fetal DNA circulating in the mother's bloodstream. It can detect chromosomal conditions and determine fetal sex with over 99% accuracy as early as 10 weeks. This is currently the earliest and most accurate method for finding out your baby's gender.

Cost: -,000+ depending on insurance and provider. Many insurance plans now cover NIPT.

Anatomy Ultrasound (20-Week Scan)

The mid-pregnancy ultrasound at 18-22 weeks is when most parents find out the gender. A trained sonographer can visualize the external genitalia with 95-99% accuracy, though position and cooperation of the baby can affect visibility.

At-Home Gender Test Kits

Several companies (SneakPeek, Peekaboo, etc.) offer at-home blood collection kits. You prick your finger, send the sample to a lab, and get results in a few days. These tests look for Y chromosomes in the mother's blood. Accuracy claims range from 90-99%, but careful sample collection is critical to avoid contamination.

Deep Dive: Entertainment Methods

Chinese Gender Calendar

The Chinese gender predictor chart (Qing Dynasty chart) claims to predict gender based on the mother's lunar age and month of conception. Despite being over 700 years old, statistical analysis shows it performs no better than a coin flip. Read our full analysis.

Nub Theory

The nub theory has the strongest scientific backing among entertainment methods. Multiple studies have found 70-91% accuracy at 12-14 weeks when interpreted by experienced professionals. However, accuracy drops significantly in real-world, at-home interpretation. Full nub theory guide.

Ramzi Theory

Despite its popularity, the Ramzi method lacks peer-reviewed evidence. The original study was never published in a medical journal, and no independent research has replicated the 97% accuracy claim. Full Ramzi theory guide.

Heart Rate Myth

The belief that fetal heart rate predicts gender (above 140 bpm = girl, below = boy) has been thoroughly debunked. A 2018 meta-analysis of over 10,000 pregnancies found zero correlation between fetal heart rate and sex. This is one of the most persistent -- and incorrect -- pregnancy myths.

Old Wives' Tales

Morning sickness, cravings, belly shape, skin changes -- these traditional signs have been passed down for generations. They're fun to track, but none have any scientific validity. Our predictor includes a 16-question old wives' tales quiz for entertainment. See all 15 tales.
