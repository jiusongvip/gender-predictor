When Can You Find Out Your Baby's Gender? A Week-by-Week Timeline

Updated August 2026 — By Gender Predictor Editorial Team

Finding out your baby's gender is one of the most anticipated moments of pregnancy. The question on every expectant parent's mind: how soon can I know? The answer depends on which method you choose, and there are more options than most people realize.

The Complete Timeline

From 6 Weeks to 20+ Weeks

Week 6-8

Early Ultrasound (Confirmation Scan)

At this stage, the external genitalia have not yet formed. You can see the gestational sac and heartbeat, but gender determination is medically impossible. However, this is when the Ramzi theory claims to work, based on placenta placement.

Week 10

NIPT (Non-Invasive Prenatal Testing)

This is the earliest scientifically reliable method. A simple blood draw from the mother analyzes fetal DNA fragments in her bloodstream. NIPT screens for chromosomal conditions and can determine fetal sex with over 99% accuracy. Results typically take 5-10 days.

Week 11-14

Nub Theory Window

Between 11 and 14 weeks, the genital tubercle (nub) is visible on ultrasound. According to nub theory, the angle of the nub may indicate sex. An angle above 30 degrees suggests a boy. This method requires a clear profile image and an experienced sonographer.

Week 12-14

CVS (Chorionic Villus Sampling)

CVS is a diagnostic test that samples placental tissue. It can definitively determine gender through chromosomal analysis. However, it carries a small risk of miscarriage (about 1 in 500) and is typically only offered when there is a medical indication.

Week 15-20

Amniocentesis

Like CVS, amniocentesis provides definitive chromosomal results including gender. It carries a small miscarriage risk (about 1 in 900) and is reserved for cases where diagnostic information is medically necessary.

Week 18-20

Anatomy Scan (Level 2 Ultrasound)

This is the standard when most parents find out. The sonographer examines fetal anatomy in detail, including external genitalia. Accuracy is 95-99% when performed by an experienced professional. The baby needs to be in a cooperative position.

At-Home Gender Tests: Buyer Beware

Several companies sell at-home blood tests claiming to determine gender as early as 6-8 weeks. These are not FDA-approved and their accuracy is inconsistent. Some independent tests have found error rates exceeding 50%. If you want early, reliable results, ask your doctor about NIPT.

How to Read Your Ultrasound for Gender

At the 18-20 week anatomy scan, the sonographer looks for three key signs:

  • Male: The penis and scrotum are visible between the legs. The "turtle sign" (penis protruding from between the legs in profile) is a classic male marker.
  • Female: The labia appear as three parallel lines (the "hamburger sign"). No protruding structures between the legs.
  • Uncertain: If the baby's legs are crossed, the cord is between the legs, or the genitals are swollen, the sonographer may not be able to determine gender with confidence.

What If You Do Not Want to Know?

About 10-15% of expectant parents choose not to find out the gender before birth. Let your sonographer know ahead of time so they can avoid showing or mentioning gender during the scan. Some couples ask the sonographer to write the gender on a card and seal it in an envelope — giving them the option to find out later.

Which Method Is Right for You?

For most low-risk pregnancies, the 20-week anatomy scan is the standard milestone. If you want the earliest possible answer, NIPT at 10 weeks is the most accurate option available to most expectant parents.

At-home gender prediction kits like SneakPeek claim to work as early as 6 weeks by detecting Y chromosomes in maternal blood. These tests report 99% accuracy in clinical studies, but contamination from male DNA in the home environment can cause false boy results.

Why the Wait Feels So Long

Between the positive pregnancy test and the anatomy scan, there are roughly 14 to 16 weeks of uncertainty. That is a long time when all you want is to know. This is exactly why prediction methods like the Chinese gender calendar and old wives' tales are so popular. They do not replace medical testing, but they make the wait feel a little shorter.
