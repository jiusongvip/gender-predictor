Pregnancy Symptoms: Boy vs Girl

Every pregnancy is different, but old wives' tales have long claimed that certain symptoms can predict whether you're carrying a boy or girl. Here's what the tales say — and what science actually shows.

Morning Sickness

Myth vs Science

Myth: Severe morning sickness means girl; mild or none means boy.
Science: A 2017 study found that women carrying girls were slightly more likely to have severe nausea, but the difference is small and not predictive for any individual pregnancy.

Food Cravings

Sweet vs Salty

Myth: Craving sweets means girl; craving salty/savory means boy.
Science: No evidence. Cravings are linked to hormonal changes and nutritional needs, not fetal gender.

Belly Shape

Myth: Carrying high means girl; carrying low means boy.
Science: Belly position depends on your body type, muscle tone, and the baby's position — not gender.

Heart Rate

Myth: Above 140 bpm means girl; below 140 means boy.
Science: Multiple large studies found zero correlation between fetal heart rate and gender.

Skin and Hair

Myth: Girls "steal their mother's beauty" causing acne and dull hair.
Science: Pregnancy hormones cause skin and hair changes regardless of the baby's gender.

Why Do These Myths Persist?

The Role of Confirmation Bias

Gender prediction myths survive because of confirmation bias. If a mother carries high and has a girl, she remembers "the tale was right." If she carries high and has a boy, she dismisses it. Over time, "hits" are remembered and "misses" forgotten.
