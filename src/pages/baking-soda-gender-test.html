Baking Soda Gender Test: Complete Guide

The baking soda gender test is one of the most popular DIY prediction methods. It is simple, cheap, and produces a visible chemical reaction — but does it actually predict your baby's gender?

How to Do the Test

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Collect first-morning urine in a clean cup.
  2. Add 1 tablespoon of baking soda to a separate cup.
  3. Pour the urine over the baking soda.
  4. Watch for a reaction.

What the Results Mean

Fizz vs No Reaction

  • Fizzes and bubbles: The myth says you're having a boy.
  • No reaction or slight fizz: The myth says you're having a girl.

The Science

Chemistry Behind the Reaction

Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) reacts with acids to produce carbon dioxide gas — the fizzing you see. Urine acidity varies with diet, hydration, and health — not fetal gender. There is zero scientific evidence supporting this test.

Accuracy: ~50%

Same as a coin flip. If you get the right answer, it is luck, not science.

Safety

Do not ingest the mixture. Perform in a ventilated area. The test is safe when done as described but provides no medical information.

Why Urine pH Varies

Your urine’s acidity level changes throughout the day and from day to day based on:

  • Diet: High-protein diets make urine more acidic (more fizz). Vegetarian diets make it more alkaline.
  • Hydration: Dehydrated urine is more concentrated and acidic.
  • Time of day: First-morning urine is typically more acidic.
  • Supplements: Vitamin C and certain medications increase acidity.
  • Infections: UTIs can alter urine pH significantly.

None of these factors are related to your baby’s gender. The fizzing (or lack of it) tells you about your urine chemistry — nothing more. For a detailed comparison with other methods, see our complete baking soda test guide with more science and history.
