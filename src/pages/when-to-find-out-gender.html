When Can You Find Out Your Baby's Gender?

The honest week-by-week answer depends entirely on how you look. Fun guesses can start before you conceive; the earliest reliable answer is a blood test at about 10 weeks; and the standard confirmation is the 18 to 20 week anatomy scan. Use the calculator to see where you are right now.

Quick Answer

10 weeks — NIPT blood test (>99%, the earliest reliable answer). 12–14 weeks — nub theory (a good guess). 18–20 weeks — anatomy scan (95–99%, the usual reveal). Diagnostic CVS (10–13) and amnio (15–20) are ~99%+ but only done for medical reasons. Every no-equipment method works anytime but only ever guesses.

10 Weeks: The Earliest Reliable Answer (NIPT)

A cell-free DNA blood test, often taken with your first-trimester screening, looks for male Y-chromosome material in your blood. Where local law allows sex disclosure, it reports gender with better than 99% accuracy from about 10 weeks — earlier than any ultrasound can be trusted.

12–14 Weeks: Nub Theory (Best Early Guess)

Before the anatomy scan, the genital tubercle angle is the most meaningful ultrasound clue. Read the full nub guide for the angle method and honest accuracy.

18–20 Weeks: The Anatomy Scan (Standard Reveal)

The mid-pregnancy scan shows the genitals directly and is the moment most parents finally learn. Position matters — a baby with crossed legs can hide the view — but accuracy is 95–99%.

What If You Do Not Want to Know?

Some parents keep it a surprise. Every method here is optional, and you can simply enjoy the guess for fun and find out at birth. Our reveal ideas work great for a surprise, too.

Frequently Asked Questions

How early can you find out the sex?

Medically, NIPT from about 10 weeks. The anatomy scan confirms at 18–20 weeks, and nub theory gives an early guess at 12–14.

How accurate is the 12-week scan?

The nub method agrees with the later scan roughly 70–90% on a clear image, easier for girls — a good guess, not a confirmation.

Earliest blood test for gender?

Cell-free DNA NIPT from ~10 weeks, >99% where disclosure is legal.

Can the anatomy scan be wrong?

Rarely. It's 95–99%; position, the cord, or swollen labia can cause an occasional misread that gets re-checked.
