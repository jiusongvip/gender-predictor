Ramzi Theory: Boy vs Girl Explained

Ramzi theory claims you can predict your baby's gender at just 6-8 weeks by looking at where the placenta attaches in the uterus.

What Ramzi Theory Says

Left vs Right Placenta

  • Placenta on the right: Boy.
  • Placenta on the left: Girl.

Dr. Saad Ramzi published a 2011 study on genderdistribution.com claiming 97.2% accuracy based on 5,376 pregnancies.

The Controversy

Never Peer-Reviewed

The study was never peer-reviewed or published in a medical journal. It appeared only on a personal website. Most OB/GYNs and radiologists do not accept Ramzi theory as valid.

How to Identify Placenta Position

On your early ultrasound, the placenta appears as a thicker, brighter area along one side of the uterus. But note: mirrored vs true images matter. Transvaginal ultrasounds are not mirrored; transabdominal ultrasounds sometimes are. This confusion alone makes Ramzi theory unreliable for the average person.

Bottom Line

Ramzi theory is interesting but unproven. Do not rely on it for any important decisions. Wait for NIPT or the anatomy ultrasound for confirmed results.

The Mirror Image Problem

One of the biggest issues with Ramzi theory is whether your ultrasound image is mirrored or true. Transvaginal ultrasounds show the true orientation — what is on the left side of the image is actually on the left side of your body. However, transabdominal ultrasounds are sometimes mirrored, meaning left and right are swapped. If you cannot confirm whether your image is mirrored, you cannot reliably apply Ramzi theory.

What Actual Doctors Say

Most OB/GYNs and radiologists do not accept Ramzi theory. The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) does not endorse any early ultrasound-based gender prediction method. Dr. Ramzi’s study remains unpublished in any peer-reviewed journal.

Ramzi vs Other Ultrasound Methods

Compared to nub theory (which looks at genital development directly), Ramzi theory looks at placental location — a secondary characteristic with no proven link to gender. Nub theory at 12+ weeks has a stronger informal track record than Ramzi theory at 6-8 weeks. For a complete methods comparison, see our detailed Ramzi theory guide and accuracy comparison page.
