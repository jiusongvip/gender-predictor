Pregnancy Symptoms: Boy vs Girl

From belly shape to cravings to whether your skin glows or breaks out, people have read pregnancy symptoms as clues to sex for centuries. Here is the folklore, the science behind each sign, and the one time symptoms genuinely matter.

Quick Answer

Symptoms reflect your hormones, body and diet — not whether the baby carries XX or XY. The folk splits (nausea and sweets = girl; carrying low and salty cravings = boy) are charming but sit at about chance. Severe vomiting can matter for your health (see a doctor), but as a sex predictor, symptoms do not beat a coin flip.

Boy Signs vs Girl Signs at a Glance

Said to mean Boy 🔵

  • Carrying low
  • Salty / savoury cravings
  • Minimal morning sickness
  • Clear skin, thick hair
  • Warm feet
  • Fewer mood swings

Said to mean Girl 🌸

  • Carrying high
  • Sweet cravings
  • Severe morning sickness
  • Dull skin or breakouts
  • Cold feet
  • Heart rate above 140

Morning Sickness

Belief: awful nausea means a girl. The science: one large study did find slightly higher nausea rates with female fetuses, but the effect is small and inconsistent between individuals — nowhere near enough to predict a single pregnancy. Nausea tracks your hormone surge, not the baby’s sex.

Sweet vs Salty Cravings

Cravings are linked to hormonal changes and possibly nutrient needs, not chromosomes. No study has shown sweets-for-girl or pickles-for-boy holds up. If you suddenly want kimchi and cake in the same hour, that is pregnancy, not a gender signal.

Belly Shape

High or low, round or wide, depends on your height, core muscle tone, how many pregnancies you’ve had and baby’s position. Two people with the same-sex baby can carry totally differently, which is why the “low means boy” rule falls apart on contact with real bodies.

Heart Rate

The “above 140 means a girl” idea has been directly tested in several studies and consistently fails — male and female fetal heart rates overlap almost completely. Read more in the old wives’ tales guide.

Skin, Hair and Mood

The “a girl steals your glow” saying attributes your own skin and hair changes to your daughter, and the “easy pregnancy means a boy” idea the reverse. Both are pure anecdote driven by hormones that vary from person to person, not by fetal sex.

Why Do These Myths Persist? Confirmation Bias

There are so many overlapping folk rules that almost any pregnancy seems to “fit” one answer in hindsight, and we remember the stories where it “worked” and forget the ones where it didn’t. That selective memory is why ancient symptom lists feel uncannily accurate long after they should have been abandoned.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can pregnancy symptoms tell me the sex?

Not reliably. They reflect your hormones, body and diet, not the baby’s chromosomes, so they perform at about chance.

Does severe morning sickness mean a girl?

There’s a weak, inconsistent hint in studies, but it’s nowhere near enough to predict an individual baby’s sex.

Why do the myths seem to fit?

Confirmation bias — enough overlapping rules that any pregnancy “matches” one way in hindsight.

When can I actually confirm?

NIPT from ~10 weeks or the 18–20 week anatomy scan. Symptoms are for self-awareness, not sex.
