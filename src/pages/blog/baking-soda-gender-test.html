Baking Soda Gender Test: Does It Really Work?

Published August 7, 2026

Among all the DIY baby gender prediction methods, the baking soda gender test is one of the most popular — and the messiest. It is a simple at-home experiment that claims to reveal your baby's sex based on a chemical reaction between your urine and baking soda. But does it actually work?

What Is the Baking Soda Gender Test?

A Simple Chemical Reaction

The baking soda gender test is a DIY method where you mix a small amount of your first-morning urine with baking soda. According to the myth:

  • If it fizzes and bubbles like soda — you are supposedly having a boy.
  • If there is no reaction or only slight fizzing — you are supposedly having a girl.

How to Do the Test at Home

While we do not recommend relying on this test for medical information, here is how people typically perform it:

  1. Collect your first-morning urine in a clean, disposable cup.
  2. Add about one tablespoon of baking soda to a separate clean cup.
  3. Pour the urine over the baking soda.
  4. Watch for a reaction — fizzing, bubbling, or no change.

Safety note: Do not ingest the mixture. Perform the test in a well-ventilated area and dispose of materials properly. Try our free online gender predictor for a mess-free alternative.

What Does Science Say?

The baking soda gender test has no scientific basis. Here is why:

  • Acid-base reaction: Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) reacts with acids to produce carbon dioxide gas — the bubbles you see. Urine pH varies based on diet, hydration, time of day, and overall health, not fetal gender.
  • Fetal gender does not change urine pH: There is no mechanism by which a fetus's sex chromosomes would alter the acidity of the mother's urine.
  • Variability: The same person could get different results on different days depending on what they ate or drank.

Accuracy Rate

Like most old wives' tale methods, the baking soda test has approximately a 50% accuracy rate — the same as a coin flip. If you get it right, it is luck, not science.

Why Is It So Popular?

Despite having no scientific validity, the baking soda test remains popular because:

  • It is cheap — baking soda costs pennies.
  • It is fast — results are immediate.
  • It feels scientific — a visible chemical reaction seems more "real" than subjective symptom-based predictions.
  • Social media has amplified it — videos of fizzing baking soda tests get millions of views.
