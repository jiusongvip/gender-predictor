Nub Theory: Boy vs Girl at 12 Weeks

Nub theory is the most popular ultrasound-based gender prediction method. It claims you can identify your baby's gender as early as 11-14 weeks by looking at the angle of the genital tubercle (the "nub").

How to Read the Nub

Step-by-Step Guide

  • Get a clear profile view of the baby.
  • Find the genital tubercle — a small protrusion between the legs.
  • Measure the angle relative to the spine.
  • Angle > 30 degrees: Likely boy.
  • Angle < 30 degrees: Likely girl.

Boy vs Girl Nub: Key Differences

Boy nub: Points upward at a steep angle, often straight or slightly curved. At 12+ weeks, may show the beginnings of male genital development.

Girl nub: Points horizontally or slightly downward, parallel to the spine. Remains flat and does not rise to form the penile shape.

Accuracy Factors

What Affects Nub Theory Reliability

Accuracy depends on: the baby's position (must be perfect profile), gestational age (better at 13-14 weeks than 11-12), image quality, and the reader's experience. Informal studies suggest 70-90% when conditions are ideal.

When to Get Confirmation

Before the 18-20 week anatomy ultrasound, do not make any decisions based on nub theory alone. It is not a substitute for medical confirmation.

Tips for Getting a Better Nub View

The quality of your nub theory reading depends almost entirely on the ultrasound image. Here are tips to improve your chances:

  • Ask for a profile view: Tell your sonographer you would like a clear side profile for your records. The nub is only visible in a perfect sagittal (side) view.
  • Timing matters: The best window is 12 weeks 0 days to 14 weeks 0 days. Before 12 weeks, the nub may not be developed enough. After 14 weeks, it may have already transformed.
  • Look for a full-body shot: You need to see the entire spine from head to rump to measure the angle accurately.
  • Zoom in: The nub is tiny — often just a few millimeters. A high-resolution image helps.

Nub Theory: Common Mistakes

Many nub theory misreadings happen because people mistake other structures for the nub:

  • The umbilical cord can sometimes look like a pointing-up nub.
  • The leg or foot can create a protrusion that looks like male anatomy.
  • A curled baby makes it impossible to get a proper angle measurement.
