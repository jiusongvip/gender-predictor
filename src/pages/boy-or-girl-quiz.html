Quick Answer

This quick quiz leans on the same folk rules as the longer version: signs like carrying low, salty cravings and clear skin point “boy”; severe sickness, sweet cravings and a higher heart rate point “girl.” Fun to play — but each rule has been tested and none beats a coin flip. Your real odds start at roughly 51–52% boy by nature.

Signs That Point to Boy

  • Carrying the baby low
  • Craving salty or savoury foods and pickles
  • Little or no morning sickness
  • Skin stays clear and hair thickens
  • Warm, normal feet (not cold)
  • The dad packs on a few extra pounds

Signs That Point to Girl

  • Carrying the baby high
  • Craving sweets and fruit
  • Strong morning sickness, especially early
  • Dull skin or breakouts
  • Fetal heart rate above 140 bpm
  • Noticeably moodier than usual

The History of Gender Folklore

These rules travel across cultures and centuries — from ancient Chinese and Egyptian methods to European and Latin American sayings — because every generation looks for a way to share in the anticipation. The complete guide to 15 old wives’ tales traces where each sign came from.

Boy or Girl: What Are Your Real Odds?

Apart from the fun rules, biology gives a near even split, tilted very slightly toward boys (about 51–52% of births worldwide). No symptom changes that. To actually learn the sex, the reliable routes are NIPT at ~10 weeks or the anatomy scan, with the nub theory as the best early ultrasound guess.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the boy or girl quiz real?

It's folklore, not prediction. The signs it uses don't reliably distinguish sex, so expect coin-flip accuracy.

Which symptoms mean a boy?

Classically carrying low, salty cravings, less sickness, clear skin and warm feet — all with no scientific backing.

Which symptoms mean a girl?

Severe sickness, sweet cravings, carrying high, dull skin, a high heart rate and moodiness — folklore again.

What are the real odds?

Close to even, with slightly more boys born (about 51–52%). Symptoms don't change it.
